IMNCI general danger signs

Any one sign in a child 2 months–5 years means urgent referral or admission.

  • Not able to drink or breastfeed
  • Vomits everything
  • Has had convulsions (or convulsing now)
  • Lethargic or unconscious

Fast breathing (count for a full minute)

Under 2 months≥60 /min
2–12 months≥50 /min
12 months–5 years≥40 /min

Young infant (<2 months) possible serious bacterial infection: not feeding well, convulsions, fast breathing ≥60, severe chest indrawing, temperature ≥37.5 °C or <35.5 °C, movement only when stimulated.

Source: WHO IMCI chart booklet; Nepal CB-IMNCI programme

  • WHO anaemia cut-offs (2024): 6–23 months <10.5 · 24–59 months <11.0 · 5–11 y <11.5 · 12–14 y <12.0 g/dL (adjust for altitude).
  • Creatinine (mg/dL): infant 0.2–0.4 · child 0.3–0.7 · adolescent 0.5–1.0 (neonatal values reflect the mother's for the first days).

ECG analysis

Upload a photo or scan of a 12-lead ECG for a systematic AI read, or enter your own measurements for a rule-based interpretation.

Classic patterns

  • Hyperkalaemia: peaked T → flat P, long PR → wide QRS → sine wave.
  • Hypokalaemia: flat T, U waves, ST depression, long QU.
  • Pericarditis: diffuse concave ST elevation with PR depression; PR elevation in aVR.
  • PE: sinus tachycardia most common; S1Q3T3, RBBB, right axis, T inversion V1–V4.
  • Wellens: deep or biphasic T in V2–V3, pain-free: critical proximal LAD stenosis.
  • De Winter: upsloping ST depression with tall T in V1–V6: LAD occlusion equivalent.
  • Brugada type 1: coved ST elevation ≥2 mm with T inversion in V1–V2.

Sources: AHA/ACCF/HRS ECG standardisation recommendations (2009); Fourth Universal Definition of MI (ESC/ACC/AHA/WHF, 2018).
